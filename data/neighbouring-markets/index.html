Neighbouring markets

Net electricity imports and exports, and the capacity of the links with Greece and Bulgaria

Data through 27 October 2026. Updated automatically each morning from EPİAŞ (last check 7 November 2026, 13:43, Istanbul time).

Türkiye’s grid runs in sync with the continental European grid. Türkiye is not part of EU market coupling, so capacity on its borders with Greece and Bulgaria is sold in separate auctions. This page shows how much electricity Türkiye imports and exports overall, and how much capacity is listed on the links with its two EU neighbours. Prices on the other side of these borders are not on this page yet.

Net imports and exports

Net imports are the electricity brought in minus the electricity sent out, across all borders, as reported by EPİAŞ. A positive bar means Türkiye imported more than it exported that day, a negative bar means it exported more. The data starts in 2018 and does not split the flow by border or into gross imports and gross exports. Use the buttons to change the time step.

Over the full years in the data, Türkiye was a net importer in 2022 and 2023 and a net exporter in 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2024 and 2025. The month with the largest net imports was January 2023 (0.66 TWh); the month with the largest net exports was August 2026 (0.66 TWh).

Capacity on the Bulgarian and Greek links

EPİAŞ lists a capacity for each direction of the two borders: Bulgaria to Türkiye (BG→TR), Türkiye to Bulgaria (TR→BG), Greece to Türkiye (GR→TR) and Türkiye to Greece (TR→GR). The chart shows the daily average of the hourly values. Capacity is what can be scheduled, not what flows: an interconnector can carry much less than its capacity or none at all.

In 2026 the listed capacity averages 356 MW from Bulgaria and 189 MW to Bulgaria, and 177 MW from Greece and 96 MW to Greece. In every year since 2019 the capacity towards Türkiye has been higher than the capacity out of it, on both borders.

Other borders

Bulgaria and Greece are not Türkiye’s only electricity neighbours. A CIGRE country presentation lists lines to Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan (Nakhchivan), Iran, Iraq and Syria as well, and its 2019 flow table shows import and export volumes only for Bulgaria, Greece and Georgia. Exports to Syria have since started: Enerdata reports 281 MW in 2025 and a target of about 900 MW.

These flows are inside the net import and export chart above, because EPİAŞ reports one total for all borders. They are missing from the capacity chart and from any breakdown by country, because the capacity series we use comes through a tool (eptr2) that only accepts the Bulgarian and Greek directions, and we have not yet found a daily source by country for the other borders. We are checking whether the EPİAŞ platform lists more directions; if it does, they will be added here.

Notes

  • Source: EPİAŞ Şeffaflık Platformu, through eptr2. Net imports are the total across all borders (“net import” in the real-time series). Capacity is the series “Hat Kapasiteleri” (Interconnection Line Capacities), which EPİAŞ describes as showing total capacity and available capacity for the interconnections.
  • Definition of the capacity series: the platform publishes a total and an available capacity. The available figure has been zero since 2024, so we show only the total. Whether the total is the physical limit of the line, the capacity offered in auctions or the capacity allocated is still to be confirmed against EPİAŞ’s documentation; we therefore do not compute how much of it is used.
