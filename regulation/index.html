Market timeline

What changed the structure of the Turkish electricity market, from a state monopoly to storage and remote control

47 changes that shaped how the Turkish power market works today, from the state monopoly to storage and remote control. Each card has one tag: Liberalisation (how the market was opened up), Power market (how electricity is priced, traded and settled), Generation (who may build and on what terms), Grid and system operation and Market transition (renewable reforms, storage, certificates, carbon pricing and electric vehicles). Laws and regulations appear only as small tags on a card, and each card says where it was read. The method page explains the source labels and what was not read, and institutions says who does what.

WarningNot legal advice, and not complete

This is a reading aid built from public pages. It covers milestones, not every amendment, and dates are those shown on the pages read. Check the official text (Resmî Gazete, mevzuat.gov.tr, EPDK) before relying on an entry.

  1. 1970s

  2. 1970

    Liberalisation

    A state monopoly

    Türkiye Elektrik Kurumu (TEK) was created with a monopoly over generation, transmission, distribution and supply.

    Electricity Authority Law (1312)Source: Law-firm sector report

  3. 1980s

  4. 1980s–90s

    Liberalisation

    Private investors enter through build-operate-transfer

    Before the market law, private participation came through build-operate-transfer, build-operate and transfer-of-operating-rights models, inside a sector still run by the state utility.

    Source: Law-firm sector report

  5. 1990s

  6. 1994

    Liberalisation

    The state utility is split

    TEK was divided into TEAŞ (generation and transmission) and TEDAŞ (distribution), which became the national distribution monopoly.

    Source: Law-firm sector report

  7. 2000s

  8. Mar 2001

    Liberalisation

    A regulator and the first market law

    The Electricity Market Law (4628) created the independent regulator EPDK and introduced licences, the eligible consumer and a market financial settlement centre. According to the EPİAŞ handbook, the state utility TEAŞ was split at the same time into generation (EÜAŞ), transmission (TEİAŞ) and wholesale companies.

    Electricity Market Law (4628)Source: Official text reproduced, EPİAŞ market handbook

  9. 2001

    Grid and system operation

    TEİAŞ (TSO) created

    TEİAŞ was formed as the state transmission company and the system operator (TSO) when the state utility was unbundled. It owns and operates the national transmission grid and is responsible for balancing and load dispatch.

    Source: Law-firm note on TEİAŞ, EPİAŞ market handbook

  10. May 2001

    Liberalisation

    A gas market law

    The Natural Gas Market Law (4646), enacted on 2 May 2001, set the framework for a regulated, competitive gas market under the same regulator, EPDK.

    Natural Gas Market Law (4646)Source: Thesis abstract on gas regulation

  11. 2001–2026

    Liberalisation

    Consumers gain the right to choose, in steps

    The 2001 law let consumers above 9 million kWh a year choose their supplier, and EPDK has set the limit every year since. The aim was a soft landing: the 2004 strategy froze the limit at 7.8 GWh until 2009 and tied it to transition contracts covering at least 85% of forecast load for those who could not yet choose, with full opening targeted for 2011. The limit then fell to 3,600 kWh in 2016, 1,200 kWh in 2021 and 500 kWh in 2026.

    Electricity Market Law (4628)Source: Competition Authority sector report, News report, 2026 limit

  12. 2003

    Power market

    Financial settlement begins

    The 2003 Financial Settlement Communiqué started pricing the electricity that autoproducers fed into and took from the system, in three time periods, according to the EPİAŞ handbook. A legal commentary dates the opening of the electricity market to 1 April 2003.

    Source: EPİAŞ market handbook, Legal commentary

  13. Mar 2004

    Liberalisation

    Strategy for the 2000s: privatise distribution first

    A strategy paper set the order of reform: sell distribution first, then generation, create portfolio generation companies and replace cost-based tariffs with an equalisation mechanism.

    Source: Strategy paper, Ministry copy, TEPAV note summarising it

  14. Nov 2004

    Power market

    First balancing and settlement rules

    The first Balancing and Settlement Regulation (DUY), of 3 November 2004, named PMUM as market operator and MYTM as system operator. Balancing and settlement under it began on 1 August 2006, with hourly instructions priced at the system marginal price and monthly settlement.

    Balancing and Settlement Regulation (DUY)Source: EPİAŞ market handbook

  15. May 2005

    Generation

    The first renewable support law

    The Renewable Energy Law (5346) introduced the YEK certificate and a support mechanism for renewable generation. The fixed price schedule per source came later, with the 2010 amendment.

    Renewable Energy Law (5346)Source: Official text reproduced

  16. 2009–2013

    Liberalisation

    Distribution is privatised

    Türkiye's distribution network was divided into 21 regions, and 20 of them were sold to private owners between 2009 and 2013 for about 12.7 billion USD in total, the first transfer (Başkent) on 28 January 2009 and the last (Toroslar) on 30 September 2013. The buyers were about ten large construction and energy groups, some holding several regions; only Kayseri stayed with its municipality.

    Source: News report, News analysis of the owners

  17. Dec 2009

    Power market

    Day-ahead planning and hourly settlement

    From 1 December 2009 the final DUY brought a day-ahead planning step that the handbook calls the first step towards the day-ahead market. Settlement became hourly and consumers began to forecast their own consumption.

    Balancing and Settlement Regulation (DUY)Source: EPİAŞ market handbook, Official text reproduced

  18. 2010s

  19. 2010–2015

    Grid and system operation

    Joined to the European grid

    In September 2010 the Turkish grid began a one-year trial of parallel operation with the continental European system, over two 400 kV lines to Bulgaria and one to Greece. Per the handbook, a permanent agreement followed on 14 April 2015.

    Source: Trade press, 2010, EPİAŞ market handbook

  20. 2011

    Generation

    YEKDEM: fixed prices, shared cost

    An amendment to the Renewable Energy Law (6094), of 29 December 2010, gave renewable plants a fixed price per source for ten years: 7.3 US cents per kWh for hydro and wind, 10.5 for geothermal and 13.3 for solar and biomass. Suppliers pay the cost together, in proportion to their sales, and the market operator calculates the total; the handbook dates the mechanism's start to 1 December 2011.

    Renewable Energy Law (5346), amended by (6094)Source: Official text reproduced, EPİAŞ market handbook

  21. 2011

    Generation

    Unlicensed generation starts

    People and companies that generate renewable electricity for their own consumption need no licence, and can sell any surplus they feed into the grid at the schedule prices for ten years; the supplier of last resort must buy it.

    Renewable Energy Law (5346), Art. 6/ASource: Official text reproduced

  22. Dec 2011

    Power market

    The day-ahead market starts

    The day-ahead market (GÖP) began on 1 December 2011 and sets the hourly day-ahead price (PTF). The collateral and advance payment system started at the same time.

    Source: EPİAŞ brochure

  23. Mar 2013

    Liberalisation

    A new main market law

    The Electricity Market Law (6446) became the market's main law. It brought in pre-licences, defined which small plants need no licence and gave EPİAŞ its legal basis.

    Electricity Market Law (6446)Source: Official text reproduced

  24. Mar 2013

    Generation

    Pre-licences

    A pre-licence lets a developer obtain the permits, approvals and land rights needed to start building before the generation licence is issued. It lasts up to 24 months, extendable by half, requires a guarantee letter, and without the permits no licence follows.

    Electricity Market Law (6446), Art. 6Source: Official text reproduced

  25. Mar 2013

    Generation

    Unlicensed generation written into the market law

    The Electricity Market Law (6446) lists the activities that need no licence: renewable plants up to 1 MW, plants that use all their output on site, municipal waste plants, micro-cogeneration and emergency generator sets. Storage and demand-side participation were added to the list in 2018.

    Electricity Market Law (6446), Art. 14Source: Official text reproduced, Law text (2018 addition)

  26. 2015

    Power market

    EPİAŞ takes over market operation

    A separate market operator, EPİAŞ, was founded in March 2015 and received a 49-year market operation licence on 1 September 2015. It took over the day-ahead and intraday markets and imbalance settlement, while TEİAŞ kept the balancing power market.

    Electricity Market Law (6446)Source: News report, EPİAŞ market handbook

  27. Jul 2015

    Power market

    Intraday market opens

    The intraday market (GİP) opened on 1 July 2015, first inside TEİAŞ, and passed to EPİAŞ with the September licence.

    Source: EPİAŞ market handbook

  28. Jan 2016

    Grid and system operation

    TEİAŞ becomes an ENTSO-E observer

    TEİAŞ states it became ENTSO-E's first and only observer member on 14 January 2016. For this site it matters because ENTSO-E data covers only Bulgaria, Greece and Georgia for Türkiye. The handbook says the first observer agreement ended in January 2019 and a new one ran from 2023 to 30 June 2026; a 14 July 2026 meeting discussed continuing it, and no later agreement was found.

    Source: TEİAŞ page, EPİAŞ market handbook

  29. Mar 2016

    Power market

    Market data goes public

    The Transparency Platform started publishing market data on 14 March 2016. It is the main data source for this site.

    Source: EPİAŞ market handbook

  30. Jun 2016

    Grid and system operation

    Grid for renewable resource areas

    An amendment to the Electricity Market Law (6719) made TEİAŞ responsible for building the transmission lines that renewable resource areas need, in line with the plants' commissioning schedules.

    Electricity Market Law (6446), amended by (6719)Source: Law text

  31. Jun 2016

    Market transition

    Renewable resource areas

    An amendment to the Electricity Market Law (6719) lets the state designate renewable energy resource areas (YEKA) and award them in competition; the winning price is paid under the support mechanism for a period set in the tender.

    Electricity Market Law (6446), amended by (6719)Source: Law text

  32. May 2017

    Generation

    Competition for wind and solar grid capacity

    When applications for a grid region exceed the capacity TEİAŞ announces, or several apply for the same site, TEİAŞ runs a competition. The lowest offer in US cents per kWh wins and may not exceed the schedule price for the source.

    Source: Regulation text (supplied)

  33. Aug 2017

    Market transition

    First large wind tender

    The first resource-area tender, for 1,000 MW of wind, was won by Siemens Gamesa, Kalyon and Türkerler, according to press reports.

    Source: News report

  34. Sep 2018

    Power market

    A spot gas market

    EPİAŞ opened the Spot Natural Gas Market on 1 September 2018, an organised market where gas shippers trade with each other around the clock and correct their imbalances.

    Source: EPİAŞ brochure

  35. May 2019

    Generation

    Unlicensed rules rewritten

    The Unlicensed Electricity Generation Regulation of 12 May 2019 keeps renewable plants up to 1 MW, or the upper limit the President sets, licence-free. Production can be offset against consumption within the same distribution region, with exceptions.

    Unlicensed Electricity Generation RegulationSource: Official text reproduced

  36. 2020s

  37. Nov 2020

    Market transition

    Certificates for renewable origin

    The market operator issues YEK-G certificates, one per MWh, as proof that supplied electricity came from renewable sources, and they can be traded.

    Renewable Energy Guarantee Certificate (YEK-G) RegulationSource: Official text reproduced

  38. 2020–2021

    Market transition

    Support prices move to lira

    An amendment to the Renewable Energy Law (7257), of December 2020, lets the President set support prices for plants commissioned after 31 December 2020, and Presidential decision 3453 (January 2021) set them in lira for plants commissioned from 1 July 2021 to the end of 2025. Press reports give 40 kuruş per kWh for hydro, 32 for wind and solar and 54 for geothermal.

    Renewable Energy Law (5346), amended by (7257)Source: News report, Law-firm note

  39. Jun 2021

    Power market

    A futures market for power

    The Power Futures Market (VEP) opened on 1 June 2021, giving traders a place to hedge months and years ahead. The first reported trade was the October 2021 base contract at 405 lira per MWh.

    Source: News report

  40. Dec 2021

    Power market

    A capacity mechanism

    The 2021 Capacity Mechanism Regulation, which replaced a 2018 one, has TEİAŞ pay selected licensed plants from a yearly budget approved by the board and recovered through transmission tariffs. Wind, hydro, solar, nuclear and plants under 50 MW are among those excluded.

    Capacity Mechanism RegulationSource: Consolidated text (supplied)

  41. Apr 2022

    Market transition

    Licences for EV charging

    Charging network operators need an EPDK licence, and charging fees must follow costs plus a reasonable margin, with EPDK able to set minimum and maximum prices.

    Charging Services RegulationSource: Law-firm note

  42. Jul 2022

    Market transition

    Pre-licences tied to storage

    An amendment to the Electricity Market Law (7417) lets companies that commit to building storage obtain a wind or solar pre-licence up to the storage capacity, outside the competition rules, and the electricity can benefit from YEKDEM.

    Electricity Market Law (6446), amended by (7417)Source: Law-firm note

  43. May 2023

    Market transition

    Lira support indexed to the dollar

    Presidential Decision 7189 (1 May 2023) sets lira prices for plants commissioned from 1 July 2021 to 31 December 2030, adjusted monthly to a US dollar index within floor and ceiling prices in US cents. Press reports add a separate, higher tier for wind and solar built with storage.

    Source: News report

  44. Dec 2023

    Grid and system operation

    One metering regulation

    The Metering Systems Regulation sets the meters and equipment for licensed and unlicensed generation and for consumption, how the readings are processed and shared and who is responsible, so that settlement and billing work from the same readings. It also defines smart meters and the national smart meter system (MASS).

    Metering Systems RegulationSource: Consolidated text (supplied)

  45. May 2024

    Generation

    What happens after ten years of unlicensed support

    An amendment to the Renewable Energy Law (7501) lets a plant that has finished its ten-year support term move to licensed generation if its owner asks and pays the licence fee and, whenever the hourly market price exceeds the support price, the difference as a contribution. Plants that stay unlicensed are paid for surplus at a price not above the market clearing price.

    Renewable Energy Law (5346), amended by (7501)Source: Consolidated text (supplied)

  46. Dec 2024

    Market transition

    Aggregators enter the market

    Aggregators can act in the market for grid users that are not market participants, such as unlicensed plants whose ten-year purchase guarantee has ended. They need a licence or a supply-licence endorsement.

    Aggregation RegulationSource: Law-firm note, EPİAŞ market handbook

  47. Jan 2025

    Power market

    15-minute settlement, for balancing power only

    The Balancing and Settlement Regulation now allows 15-minute periods for balancing power, no earlier than 1 January 2027. The rest of settlement stays hourly.

    Balancing and Settlement Regulation (DUY)Source: Consolidated text (supplied)

  48. Jul 2025

    Market transition

    Emissions trading and a new role for EPİAŞ

    The Climate Law (7552) creates an emissions trading system; the market operator it names is EPİAŞ, which runs the market, financial settlement and the registry.

    Climate Law (7552)Source: Official text reproduced

  49. Dec 2025

    Grid and system operation

    Storage and aggregators in system services

    Storage can take part in ancillary services and the balancing power market, and aggregators got rules for ancillary services. TEİAŞ must announce its capacity procurement at least 90 days ahead.

    Ancillary Services RegulationSource: Regulation text, Regulation text

  50. 2025–2026

    Market transition

    Storage in the support scheme

    Storage is allowed at unlicensed plants under conditions, and the energy exported from it is unpaid and counted as a contribution to YEKDEM. From October 2026, for storage at a generation plant, YEKDEM counts only stored energy that virtual records show was produced after the plant joined the scheme.

    Storage Activities RegulationYEK RegulationSource: Regulation text, News report

  51. Mar 2026

    Generation

    Connection capacity allocated by transformer centre

    TEİAŞ now publishes the room for unlicensed solar and wind transformer centre by transformer centre; press reports link it to EPDK board decision 14353 of 26 February 2026, with 1,500 MW at transmission level and 2,000 MW at distribution level. A listed capacity does not by itself give a right to connect.

    Source: News report, Law-firm guide

  52. May 2026

    Generation

    Settlement for unlicensed plants

    Board decision 14531 (5 May 2026) sets how the market operator settles unlicensed plants and the consumption points tied to them, using an unlicensed production module as the settlement unit.

    Source: Decision text (supplied)

  53. Oct 2026

    Grid and system operation

    Emergency control of unlicensed plants

    From 19 October 2026 the system operator can give emergency instructions to unlicensed plants (distribution-connected ones of 900 kW and above, through suppliers or aggregators) and to standalone storage, and an unlicensed plant that misses a load-shedding instruction is not paid for that output. Consumers who build storage above their contract power must install automatic power control and an energy cut-off.

    Unlicensed Generation RegulationBalancing and Settlement Regulation (DUY)Source: News report, Regulation text
